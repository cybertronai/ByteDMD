1. The Dominance Theorem: Rigid Beams vs. Fluid Flow

Both bounds try to find the absolute minimum geometric energy required to map variables to 2D physical distances. They both avoid NP-Hard integer bin-packing by relaxing the math, but they disagree on one fundamental law of physics: Spatial Lock-in.

The Polymatroid LP (Rigid Beams)

Because it colors an Interval Graph, the LP enforces Global Spatial Exclusivity. It treats variables as rigid steel beams extending across time. If the compiler assigns Matrix A to physical Depth 2 to make its reads cheap, that physical hardware slot is permanently locked for A's entire lifespan.

Local Density / static_opt_lb (Fluid Flow)

The Local Density bound treats memory like a fluid. It completely discards the rigid interval graph. Instead, it stops time at every individual clock tick $t$, calculates the instantaneous density of all live variables ($\rho = \text{reads}/\text{lifespan}$), and assigns them to depths strictly by their density rank.

The Teleportation Loophole: Because Local Density evaluates every tick independently, it mathematically grants the compiler the magical ability to teleport data for free.

If a heavy background array is sitting at Depth 1, and a dense temporary variable suddenly spawns, Local Density smoothly slides the background array to Depth 2 for a split second, and then magically slides it back to Depth 1 the moment the temporary dies.

Because Local Density perfectly optimizes placement tick-by-tick without enforcing the penalty of permanent commitment, it acts as a continuous relaxation. Therefore, the Polymatroid LP will always calculate a higher, strictly tighter theoretical floor.

2. The "Phase Spanner" Effect (When the Gap is Huge)

The gap between the two bounds explodes on algorithms that exhibit Temporal Heterogeneity—specifically, when long-lived background arrays span across hot bursts of inner temporaries.

Take naive_matmul or unstructured Graph walks:

  • The C accumulator matrix lives for a massive amount of time. Its global density ($\rho$) is very low.
  • The inner tmp variables live for 4 cycles and die. Their density is very high.
  • Local Density LP lets the C matrix continuously bob in and out. It pushes C to the outer orbits during the multiply, but in the microscopic gaps between loop iterations, it lets C teleport back into Depth 1.
  • Polymatroid LP realizes that C casts a continuous physical "shadow" over the whole execution. Because C cannot teleport, the LP is forced to make a brutal physical choice: permanently banish C to the outer orbits, or permanently banish the temporaries. It correctly charges a massive $O(N^3)$ volumetric energy penalty that Local Density was blind to.

When do they converge?

The bounds will converge to be nearly identical on algorithms with Perfect Temporal Homogeneity.

In a perfectly optimized tiled_matmul or a flat LayerNorm, the inner accumulators are strictly scoped. They are born, accumulated, and die within the L1 tile before the next tile begins. You manually destroy the "shadowing" effect, shattering the interval graph into disjoint cliques. In this regime, the Teleportation Loophole is never triggered, and Local Density perfectly matches the True LP.
