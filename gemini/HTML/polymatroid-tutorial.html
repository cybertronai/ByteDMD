To truly understand the Polymatroid Relaxation—and why it is such a brilliant mathematical trick for analyzing spatial memory—we have to change how we visualize cost.

Usually, when we think of a memory allocator, we think of playing a game of Jigsaw Puzzle (or Tetris). We pick up a variable, assign it to physical Slot 1, pick up another variable, assign it to Slot 2, and so on. Because a variable must be locked into its physical slot for its entire lifespan, finding the perfect packing order to minimize the geometric fetch cost is an NP-Hard combinatorial nightmare.

The Polymatroid Relaxation completely bypasses the Jigsaw Puzzle. It replaces strict physical addresses with a sequence of Parallel Universes.

Here is a tutorial-style breakdown of exactly how the math works, a representative example of the "loophole" that makes it a relaxation, and a blueprint for creating illustrative graphics for a paper or presentation.

---

Part 1: The "Toll Booth" Model (Discrete Calculus)

In your 2D grid, the cost to fetch from depth $d$ is $\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil$.

Instead of calculating the final cost of a variable based on where it lands, we use Discrete Calculus to break the 2D grid into an entry fee plus a series of concentric "Toll Booths."

  1. The Base Cost: Every read in the program pays a base entry fee of 1.
  2. Toll Booth 1 (Pushed past 1 slot): If a variable is forced out of the premium inner slot into Depth 2, it pays a marginal penalty toll: $+1$.
  3. Toll Booth 2 (Pushed past 4 slots): If it is forced out of the inner 4 slots into Depth 5, it pays the next step-up penalty: $+1$.

By manipulating the summation, the total geometric energy of your algorithm is simply the base cost plus the penalties paid by the "spillover" reads at every toll booth:

$$\text{Total Energy} = R_{total} + \sum_{c \in {1, 4, 9...}} \Delta_c \times (\text{Reads forced past capacity } c)$$

Part 2: The "Amnesia" Oracle ($M_c$)

To find the absolute minimum energy, we need to minimize the spillover at each toll booth. We do this by asking a Linear Programming (LP) Oracle a sequence of isolated questions:

"If I draw a magic circle around the ALU that holds exactly $c$ variables, what is the absolute maximum number of reads ($M_c$) I can physically pack inside it?"

Because variable lifespans form an Interval Graph (which is mathematically "Perfect"), the LP can answer this fractional packing problem instantly with perfect integer results.

The reads mathematically forced to spill over past capacity $c$ are exactly: $(R_{total} - M_c)$.

---

Part 3: The Exploit (Why is it a "Relaxation"?)

If the Oracle perfectly solves the packing problem, why is this a Lower Bound and not a realizable, physical schedule?

Here is where the magic happens. We ask the Oracle to solve $M_1$, $M_4$, and $M_9$ completely independently. We grant the math a temporary "Amnesia" between each step.

Let's look at an example trace with 3 variables to see how the LP "cheats" physical reality to find a mathematical floor.

  • Var A (Long Background Matrix): Lives $t=0 \dots 10$. Has 80 reads.
  • Var B (Short Temp): Lives $t=1 \dots 4$. Has 50 reads.
  • Var C (Short Temp): Lives $t=6 \dots 9$. Has 50 reads.
    (Note: B and C don't overlap in time, but A overlaps both).

How the Oracle calculates $M_c$:

  • $M_1$ (Capacity 1): The Oracle can pick A (80 reads). Or it can pick B + C (50 + 50 \= 100 reads, because they don't overlap and can share the slot). The Oracle picks B + C. $M_1 = 100$.
  • $M_2$ (Capacity 2): With two slots, the Oracle can take everything! It puts A in Slot 1, and B + C in Slot 2. $M_2 = 180$.

Look closely at the physical contradiction.

To minimize the penalty at Toll Booth 1 ($M_1$), the Oracle claimed that A was banished to the outer orbits, and B/C were in the premium slot.

To minimize the penalty at Toll Booth 2 ($M_2$), the Oracle claimed that A was safely sitting in the premium inner slots!

In a strict static physical layout, you cannot dynamically swap which variable gets the premium Slot 1 based on how big you pretend the cache is. A must be locked somewhere permanently.

But the mathematics of the Discrete Calculus identity assume you can swap them. We grant the compiler the magical ability to "repack" the inner cache for free at every capacity boundary. Because this hyper-optimistic free-repacking can only artificially lower the calculated cost, the resulting summation is an unbreakable, mathematically airtight Lower Bound.

(Note: In graph theory, a set-function where the optimal inner sub-sets do not strictly nest inside the larger optimal sets is called a Polymatroid, which is where the relaxation gets its name).

Part 4: The Visualization Blueprint

To make this instantly intuitive in a paper or presentation, do not plot equations. You should plot a "Marginal Spillover" Area Chart. This flawlessly translates the abstract algebraic integration into physical geometry.

You can create a two-panel graphic:

Panel 1 (Left): The Jigsaw Puzzle (Physical Lock-in)

  • Visual: Draw a Gantt chart. X-axis is time. Y-axis is Physical Depth (Slot 1, Slot 2).
  • Draw horizontal bars for variables. Draw a massive, thick blue bar for a background matrix, and lots of tiny red bars for temporaries.
  • Purpose: Visually demonstrate the "Tetris" problem. Show that because the heavy blue bar is locked into Slot 1, the red temporaries are physically forced to bump up into Slot 2 and 3, paying higher costs.

Panel 2 (Right): The Waterfall of Areas (The Polymatroid LP)

  • Axes:
  • X-Axis: Cumulative Number of Reads (from $0$ to $R_{total}$).
  • Y-Axis: The Marginal Fetch Penalty ($\Delta_c$).
  • How to draw it:
  • The Base Layer (Ground Floor): Draw a massive, wide rectangle spanning from $x=0$ to $x=R_{total}$, with a height of $1$. Label it: "Base Fetch Cost (All reads pay 1)."
  • The First Spillover (Past Cap 1): Draw a vertical dashed line at $x = M_1$. To the right of $M_1$, draw a second rectangle sitting exactly on top of the first. It spans from $x = M_1$ to $x = R_{total}$. Its height is the marginal penalty ($\Delta_1$). Label it: "Spillover past Capacity 1."
  • The Second Spillover (Past Cap 4): Draw a vertical line at $x = M_4$. To the right of $M_4$, draw a third rectangle sitting on top of the stack, spanning to $R_{total}$. Label it: "Spillover past Capacity 4."

Why this graphic is a show-stopper

It converts an NP-Hard linear algebra algorithm into pure, visual Area ($\text{Width} \times \text{Height} = \text{Reads} \times \text{Penalty}$). The total geometric area of the stacked rectangles is exactly your Total Energy Bound!

It makes the concept immediately obvious to the reader: If your algorithm has a severe bottleneck (lots of overlap), $M_1$ shrinks to the left. The "waterfall" of rectangles becomes incredibly wide, and the visual area (energy cost) explodes. If your algorithm has great spatial locality (like well-tiled matmul), $M_1$ pushes to the right, the rectangles shrink, and the energy cost visibly drops to the floor.
